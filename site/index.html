Pyometra Outcome Predictor

Estimates the probability that medical management of canine pyometra fails by day 14 — from six routine admission values. Built on an 80-dog four-arm cohort (G1 supportive, G2 PGF2α, G3 aglepristone + PGF2α, G4 ovariohysterectomy).

Not a validated clinical tool. Trained on a single-centre teaching dataset of 80 dogs with 14 failure events. Use as a teaching and discussion aid only; the attending clinician's judgement takes precedence.

How the tool works

The workflow follows the canine-parvovirus prognosis study of Nafe Monfared et al. (Front. Vet. Sci. 2025): screen admission variables, reduce them with recursive feature elimination, then compare many algorithms under repeated stratified cross-validation.

The data

Eighty dogs presenting with pyometra were assigned across four protocols (20 each) and followed to day 14. Admission demographics, vital signs, haematology, serum biochemistry and ultrasonographic uterine diameter were recorded. Baseline data are complete; the cohort is balanced across arms on every measured variable.

The model behind this predictor

An L2-penalised logistic regression for medical failure by day 14 (death, rescue ovariohysterectomy, or non-response), fitted on the using six variables that survived selection: BUN, creatinine, alkaline phosphatase, albumin, age and illness duration. Held-out discrimination was (repeated stratified 5-fold CV). Random forest and naïve Bayes performed within cross-validation noise of this model; logistic regression was kept because it is transparent and portable.

Limitations

  • Single centre, 80 dogs, 14 failure events — confidence intervals are wide and external validity is untested.
  • The surgical arm (G4) was not randomised and is perfectly separated on outcome, so protocol comparisons are descriptive, not causal.
  • On this clean dataset the model is over-confident at the extremes; treat the output as a band (low / intermediate / high), not a precise percentage.
  • No prospective or multi-centre validation. This is a methodology demonstration.
